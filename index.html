konomi-clean

Is your benchmark already in the training data?

Contamination is normally reported as one number — "3% contaminated" — which tells you nothing useful. This reports which items, at what overlap, and then the only figure anyone actually wants: what the headline score becomes once the leaked items are removed.

Containment, not Jaccard

An eval item is a few dozen words; a corpus is billions. Jaccard reads ~0 even for a verbatim copy, so it fails exactly on the case it was deployed to catch. Using it here is a real and common bug.

Per item, not one rate

A rate cannot be acted on. Every item gets a verdict and an overlap figure, and verbatim copies are identified as verbatim rather than approximated.

The impact is the finding

A headline that moves thirteen points when the leaked items are dropped was never measuring the model. Detection is the easy half.

Nothing leaves your browser. In practice you rarely have the training corpus — but you always have your own fine-tuning data, your RAG index, and last quarter's eval set, and those are where leakage most often comes from.

The same verbatim copy, scored both ways

One eval item is placed inside a corpus, and the corpus is then grown with unrelated text. Nothing about the copy changes. Watch what each measure says about it.

This is why the measure matters more than the algorithm. A contamination checker built on Jaccard gets quieter the larger the corpus is — which is the opposite of what you need, and it fails silently rather than erroring.

Verdicts

VerdictMeaning
EXACTThe normalised item occurs verbatim inside a reference document. Decided by substring match, not by the overlap threshold — a short item can be present word-for-word while its containment sits low, and a verbatim copy is the least ambiguous finding there is.
NEARContainment ≥ 0.8 — nearly all of the item's n-grams appear in the reference.
PARTIALContainment ≥ 0.3 — a substantial fragment is present. Often legitimate; worth reading.
CLEANBelow the thresholds.

Honest limits

  • You usually cannot see the training data. This checks against a corpus you hold — your own fine-tuning set, your retrieval index, a previous eval set. Absence of a finding here is not evidence that a model never saw your benchmark.
  • Word n-grams at n ≥ 8 essentially do not recur by chance, but at small n ordinary English phrasing will flag. The n is yours to set, and the default is a compromise.
  • MinHash mode estimates. It cannot report EXACT, because it has no text to substring-match — it says so in the result rather than guessing.
  • Paraphrased contamination is not caught by any n-gram method. This finds copies, not ideas.
  • The 5% mark for calling an impact "material" is a reporting convenience, not a law. The delta is the finding.
